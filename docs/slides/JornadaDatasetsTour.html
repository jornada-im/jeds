A little background

  • Jornada Basin has been an intensive research site for a long time.
  • Jornada Experimental Range (JER), established in 1912
  • Jornada Basin LTER (JRN), established in 1982
  • Many other programs collect, or have collected, data in the Jornada Basin.

Here are a few…

Data collection, management, and use have evolved

  • People, their studies and research priorities turn over
  • Funding for programs can be unsteady
  • As researchers, we’re not always perfect about data management
  • Open data is a relatively new priority
  • Managing and curating Jornada data is a continual work in progress…

Our objectives today

  • Learn what Jornada data are available and how they can be used in your research.
  • Get practical experience working with Jornada data.
  • Please take time to do the activities and ask questions. We are learning by doing!

How do we classify Jornada data?

  • Core: long-term datasets collected or directly supported by JRN and JER research staff
  • Contributed: shorter term (usually) datasets collected by any researchers funded by, or formally affiliated with, JRN and/or JER
  • Partner: datasets collected by external partners, like other federal agencies or research networks.
  • Core and contributed datasets are further organized by 3 digit study ID:
  • 210011001 = site manager, study ID, dataset ID

Long-term vegetation data

  • Primary production
  • Cover

Primary production data

  • Biomass or carbon accumulation by primary producers (plants, usually)
  • Variable: Gross or net primary production, aboveground or belowground
  • Units: g biomass · m-2 · day-1 or g C · m-2 · day-1

The Jornada NPP study

A non-destructive ANPP method for drylands

  • 1m2 long-term quadrats installed at 15 Jornada sites in 1989
  • Field datasets:
    • Replicate species harvests (dimensions & biomass)
    • Plant dimensions (only) collected in long-term quadrats
  • Allometric method to estimate biomass
  • Huenneke, Clason, and Muldavin described the method in [

Used in numerous JRN pubs & cross-site syntheses

  • NPP sites anchor many other Jornada studies
  • Meteorology, soil profile characterization, soil water/temperature, plant traits, aerial photos, terrain/elevation models, phenology, animal studies, short-term grad student projects
  • Validating new research methods (UAV/Remote sensing, models)

Jornada ANPP data

  • Jornada NPP study sites (15 sites)
  • Ecotone project (Bestelmeyer - 5 sites)
  • Global change experiment (Sala - 1 site, slightly different method)
  • Variable names differ, same units, same temporal resolution
  • Red = 15 NPP sites; Orange = Ecotone, Green = GlobalChange

Other datasets about production

  • Eddy covariance fluxes (NEE, partitioned fluxes)
  • Belowground NPP (root biomass production) - not common but not unheard-of

Plant cover

  • Cover of plant species of functional type in an area.
  • Variable: percent or fractional cover
  • Units: dimensionless

Jornada permanent quadrats

  • Plant type, cover, and density measured since 1915
  • Yearly to 1970s, break until early 90s, resuming at 5-year frequency
  • 122 locations across the Jornada Experimental Range

Useful for assessing long-term vegetation change

  • such as grass-shrub transitions
  • Also, potentially useful for biodiversity research

Jornada quadrat cover data

  • Jornada permanent quadrats (122, starting 1915)
  • Ecotone quadrats (5, starting 2005)
  • SMES (2, starting 1995)
  • Different variable names, different units, different temporal resolution, different treatments
  • Red = Perm Quads; Orange = Ecotone, Green = SMES

Weather and climate

  • Jornada met stations
  • Rain gauges
  • Soil moisture

Meteorology data

  • Data collected from weather stations, including air temperature and RH, wind speed/direction, and precipitation.

Longest-term weather stations

  • JER headquarters NOAA station. Collecting data 1914-present.
    • Data available at NCDC
    • There are other NOAA stations in the region with similar records
  • LTER weather station. Collecting data 1983-present
    • Data at EDI
    • Some data issues being worked on

NPP and Cross-scale weather stations

  • Collecting continuously since 2013, multiple frequencies
  • Data are regularly quality assured and sent to EDI
  • There are some estimated/gapfilled precipitation datasets (since 1989) too

Rain gauges

  • There is a very extensive rain gauge network with multiple gauge types, varying temporal records. Rain is very unevenly distributed in NM.
  • Monthly standard cans (cumulative)
  • Weighing gauges
  • Tipping-bucket (regular timeseries or event-based)
  • Best practices is to ask the field crew or IM team which rain gauge

Other meteorology data

  • Several wind profile weather stations
  • Individual experiments sometimes have their own rain gauge
  • Eddy covariance towers
    • Tromble watershed, UTEP Bajada & Red Lake Playa on AmeriFlux
    • JER DuRP project towers (3)
    • Army research lab - 30+ EC towers

What are spatial data & GIS?

  • (Geo)spatial data: Any data with location or geographic coordinates attached to the observation
  • Geographic information systems (GIS): Integrated computer hardware and software that store, manage, analyze, edit, output, and visualize geographic data

Why do we care at the Jornada?

  • Drylands are spatially heterogeneous
  • We work in a large area
  • We want to place our research in landscape context

The Jornada Spatial Data Catalog

  • There are well over 1000 datasets
  • Study & measurement locations
  • Instrumentation, fences, roads, boundaries, buildings
  • Physical geography
  • Vegetation and soil survey products
  • Remote sensing data
  • Model outputs
  • Only a small amount of datasets have been published or made accessible

How is it managed?

  • Somewhat decentralized and siloed
  • There are some great USDA GIS staff
  • The LTER produces lots of data
  • Researchers often manage their own pile of GIS data
  • Some data are restricted access
  • We are working out improved coordination and access methods
  • A data commons is one goal…

What is RAP?

  • https://rangelands.app
  • Allred et al. 2021, 2025, Jones et al. 2021

RAP is two things: 1) Remotely-sensed vegetation data, 2) a simple web application that puts this data at your fingertips for free.

Specifically, the data products include vegetation cover and production data layers representing various plant functional groups at various spatial scales and through time. Most data layers are available across the entire US.
